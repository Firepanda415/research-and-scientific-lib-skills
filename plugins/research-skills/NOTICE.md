Changes add scientific accuracy and resource requirements and update installation guidance. Earlier versions of this plugin integrated the upstream lifecycle hooks and their tests, and these have been removed. Codex and Claude Code load `ponytail` for coding work, read-only simplification and retirement decisions, or an explicit request. Related engineering skills conditionally load it within their existing workflow. A level named in the conversation lasts until the user names another or turns Ponytail off, and `full` applies when none is named. Companion skills respect the off state. `ponytail-review` is one-shot and leaves the level unchanged, which departs from upstream, where it replaces that level with a persistent review mode. `ponytail-audit` directs evidence-backed simplification surveys and authorized cleanup to `simplify-codebase`.

Joseph M. Williams and Joseph Bizup's *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*, 11th edition, Pearson, copyright 2014, ISBN 978-0-321-89868-5, informed the sentence-generation guidance in `research-writing-style/references/durable-prose.md` and the contextual reader diagnostics in `research-writing-style/references/prose-review.md`. The complete book was consulted on 2026-09-17. These procedures are original writing by this collection, with bibliographic and page references. Local sentence mechanisms guide generation, while document structure, paragraph function, evidence, and context guide mandatory adversarial review. Semantic checks and the user's explicit requirement to keep drafting instructions and incidental conversation out of finished prose apply throughout. The book does not itself prescribe a modern prompt-leakage review. The PDF, extracted text, illustrations, examples, exercises, and answer key are not distributed. The book's copyright and its quoted sources' rights are not covered by this collection's MIT or Creative Commons licenses. The adoption audit records which ideas were used, qualified, or excluded.

The optional [detector-evaluation workflow](skills/research-writing-style/references/detector-evaluation.md) is original collection guidance under the collection's MIT license. It supports explicitly requested rewriting and empirical detector comparisons while preserving source meaning. Its scope follows user-requested workflow changes, not a new third-party source or a claim about detector mechanisms or effectiveness. Test manuscripts, submitted variants, and account records are not bundled with the workflow.

Original editorial observations from those rewriting trials also refine the required generation and review references. They connect decisions to their evidence, capabilities to concrete operations, and qualifications to the claims they limit, and check revisions within unchanged neighboring text. The trial records, including test passages and detector results, are not distributed with the plugin. These refinements follow their respective reference files' existing licenses and do not claim a proven detector mechanism.

The shared test-necessity guidance in `ponytail` and its use in `scientific-library-review` are collection modifications. They judge tests by current user obligations and the failures that remaining checks would miss after removal. The library-review guidance also distinguishes cases by their scientific premises and separates source inventory from substantive review and executed validation.
