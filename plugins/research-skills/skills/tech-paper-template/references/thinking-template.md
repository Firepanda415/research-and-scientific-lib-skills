# Optional prompts for a method-paper skeleton

Use these prompts when a method paper's argument is unclear. Omit cells that do
not fit the genre; neither a count nor a sentence limit diagnoses scientific
quality. The same question can be answered directly in prose.

| Prompt | What it should clarify | Common issue |
|---|---|---|
| Research context | The scientific or practical question and why it matters | Generic importance without a connection to the work |
| Relevant existing result or limitation | What is known and what remains unresolved | Unsupported dismissal of prior work |
| Main insight or goal | The mechanism, result, or precise question | A label replacing an explanation |
| Necessary obstacle | Why a naive route does not establish the result | A challenge invented to justify a module |
| Method or argument | How the obstacle is handled under the assumptions | Implementation detail hiding the decisive step |
| Contribution and evidence | What the paper establishes and where the support is | An unsupported or padded claim |

For theory or explanatory work, definitions, assumptions, theorem statements,
proof ideas, and implications may replace method-oriented cells. A replication
may center the original claim, reproduced conditions, and observed agreement or
disagreement. Practical beneficiaries and framework names are not mandatory.

Preserve exact assumptions and result-sensitive definitions. Use a claim-to-source
or claim-to-evidence mapping when it reveals a real gap; explicit section-number
lists are optional. A method component can be useful infrastructure without being
a scientific contribution.

Derive a section outline from the actual argument. Do not add modules, paragraphs,
contribution bullets, or experiments to fill the template. Missing evidence can
be serious; missing template structure alone is not.
