# Drafting an Introduction

Use this reference when drafting or substantially restructuring an Introduction. Start from the available manuscript, claims, and evidence; a formal handoff record is unnecessary. Return prose when prose was requested.

Connect the research question, relevant existing work, unresolved issue, central contribution, and supporting evidence. Explain assumptions early when they determine the contribution's meaning. A method paper may use background, limitation, goal, technical obstacle, solution, and contribution as prompts. They are not six required paragraphs. Use [paper-types.md](paper-types.md) for other narrative forms.

## Examples and claim wording

Use the smallest example that preserves the distinction being explained. A real instance, clearly labeled synthetic case, or exact toy model may help. Verify equations, parameters, and outputs. An illustrative competitor failure is not observed evidence, and a toy example does not establish scaling. Reuse an example later only when it saves explanation.

State what the work establishes: a theorem under assumptions, a mechanism under comparison conditions, a replicated agreement or discrepancy, or a measured finding within a tested regime. Do not turn implementation modules or experiment volume into separate scientific contributions merely to fill a list. A contribution may be written in prose and need not carry a section number.

Keep prior-work claims supportable, result-sensitive regimes explicit, and theoretical guarantees separate from finite numerical evidence. Writing final contributions after the experiments is normal; implying exploratory findings were preregistered or hiding contradictory evidence is not.
