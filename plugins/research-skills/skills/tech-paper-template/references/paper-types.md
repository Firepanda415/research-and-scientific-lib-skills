# Common narrative axes

Choose the narrative from the contribution and evidence. These are useful
examples, not an exhaustive taxonomy or a routing gate.

| Main contribution | Useful emphasis |
|---|---|
| New method or mechanism | What changes, why it works, and the relevant comparison |
| New problem or setting | Why the formulation matters and how its assumptions differ |
| Theorem or bound | Definitions, assumptions, the result, and the proof insight |
| Explanation or negative result | The phenomenon or failed premise and what the analysis resolves |
| Replication | The original claim, faithful conditions, and what agrees or differs |
| Benchmark or measurement | What is measured, construction/measurement validity, and what is learned |
| Resource estimate | The access model, cost components, assumptions, and useful regime |
| Cross-field transfer | The structural correspondence and assumptions that survive transfer |

A paper can combine contributions when the argument remains coherent. Lead with
the part that best explains its value without automatically splitting the work.
Do not require an improvement over a leaderboard, a new framework name, a running
example, or an explicit goal paragraph. Allocate space to what readers need to
understand and assess the actual result.
