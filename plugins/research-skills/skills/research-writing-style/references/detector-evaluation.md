# Requested detector evaluation and rewriting

Use this mode when the user explicitly asks to rewrite against detector results or test whether revisions change a detector's classification. Use supplied variants or produce the requested revisions, then report measured outcomes. Ordinary writing and review do not require detector checks. A style rewrite may change sentence and paragraph organization even when the original is grammatically sound. Do not substitute a scientific audit for the requested rewriting work.

## Keep the experiment interpretable

Choose coherent passages and preserve their source locations and relevant context. Separate article prose from advertisements, running headers, tables, and extraction debris. Repair transcription before establishing the baseline. Save the exact submitted text so a later difference in context or transcription is not mistaken for a rewrite effect. If the service needs a longer passage, include adjacent relevant text and use that same passage boundary for both versions.

Test the original and revised text under the same available detector mode. Keep the exact text, sample identifier, submission time, visible settings, word count, and returned classifications or scores for every attempt. Record service or model versions only when exposed. Separate user-reported earlier results from results observed in the current test. Do not invent a percentage from a label or treat a document-level result as a sentence-level result.

For an evaluation of the skill's first-pass rewriting, save the initial alternatives before their writer sees live detector feedback. If testing whether later changes transfer to another passage, choose a held-out sample before using the development results and withhold its detector feedback until those changes are complete. A passage chosen after seeing its results is another development example. A few samples from one paper do not establish broad effectiveness.

## Rewrite and review

Use the supplied author samples, genre, and surrounding argument to choose a voice. Consider clause order, repeated exposition, paragraph hierarchy, generic framing, and the relation between qualifications and their claims. The user's request permits experimenting with wording to improve the reported classification. It does not justify invented facts, deliberate errors, fake personal experience, decorative synonym changes, or discarded technical conditions. Keep the parent's meaning and style constraints.

Review each candidate against the original before submission and delivery. Check quantities, symbols, comparisons, citations, uncertainty, scope, and the reasoning connecting claims. Read its connections to neighboring paragraphs. A lower detector score cannot rescue a revision that changes the science or becomes harder to understand. Record a needed substantive correction separately from a style revision, since it changes the content of the comparison.

For feedback-driven revisions, work in a bounded batch appropriate to the request and available usage. Save each attempt and the change being tested. Report unchanged and worse outcomes alongside improvements. Do not keep rerolling indefinitely or present only the best result. If an ordinary trial reaches its limit, deliver the tested variants and measured limits. Follow a user-requested continued experiment within its stated budget.

## Access and reporting

Use the user's authorized service and material. An instruction to run the tests in the logged-in account supplies authorization for those submissions, so do not ask again without a new scope or permission issue. It does not authorize unrelated uploads, purchases, or account changes. If access or quota prevents a test, report it as untested and still provide any completed rewriting work.

Deliver the requested revised text and a compact account of the original result, each attempted revision's result, and any fidelity problem. Keep experimental notes outside paste-ready prose. State whether results came from isolated passages or the full document. A score change is an observed response to a particular submission, not proof of authorship, a known detector mechanism, or a guarantee about a future submission. Clearly separate editorial hypotheses from measured results.
