# Reusable evidence records

Use a ledger when a broad inquiry, contradiction, or later handoff benefits from
claim-level records. A narrow answer normally needs only citations and relevant
source locations. Do not make a ledger a prerequisite for using a source.

A compact record can contain:

| Claim | Source and location/version | Support and assumptions | Open issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| The statement being used | Paper section, theorem, artifact, or code path | Direct support, inference, contradiction, or incomplete evidence | What could change the conclusion, if any |

Add an identifier only when other records need to refer to it. Add dates,
counterevidence, source independence, or a decision consequence when those facts
matter. Do not fill empty confidence or follow-up fields mechanically.

Keep distinct claims separable when their support differs. One source record can
support several claims by reference; do not duplicate the source or its full
contents for each claim. Preserve enough detail to revisit result-sensitive
assumptions and comparisons.

For novelty or historical lineage, distinguish the earliest located formulation,
closest relevant work, changed assumptions, and the actual search boundary.
"Not found" in a bounded search does not establish novelty. A publication reports
a claim; it does not establish independent mathematical verification.

Relationships such as supports, contradicts, depends on, or shares evidence with
should express an inspected connection or a clearly labeled inference. A map's
hierarchy is navigation, not proof.
